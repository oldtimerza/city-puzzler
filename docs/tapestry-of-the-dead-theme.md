# Tapestry Of The Dead

## Premise

The player is the Keeper of the Final Record, a sacred archivist who travels between the islands of the gods. Their work is not to build a city, but to arrange stars and weave their connections so the lives of the dead will never be forgotten.

Every completed puzzle restores a piece of an illustrated celestial history. An empty field of darkness becomes a named scene in the heavens. Across the campaign, these scenes connect into the gods' enormous Tapestry of the Dead.

## Player Fantasy

The Keeper is careful, compassionate, and authoritative. The player is preserving a life rather than optimizing infrastructure. Puzzle feedback should feel like archival restoration: stars settle into their rightful places, threads illuminate, and an account becomes whole.

Avoid city-builder language such as districts, production, utilities, construction, or expansion. Prefer remembrance, record, soul, constellation, chapter, thread, witness, rite, fate, and heaven.

## Laws Of Remembrance

### Every Soul Has A Shape

Each hero's life becomes a distinct constellation arrangement: a blade, crown, ship, beast, lantern, gate, or abstract sigil. A puzzle's target shape is a visual record of that life, not an arbitrary board pattern.

### Every Star Carries Meaning

Stars represent qualities and events, including courage, wisdom, sacrifice, love, betrayal, oath, grief, and mercy. A hero can only be placed among stars that reflect their life.

### Threads Of Fate Obey Boundaries

Constellation connections may not cross, may need to pass through a divine landmark, may remain within a fixed celestial distance, or may follow a prescribed path through the sky.

### Souls Affect One Another

Companions want adjoining constellations. Rivals cannot share stars. Families form chains. Enemies may be required to face one another across the heavens. These relationships make the record a shared history rather than a set of isolated placements.

### The Record Must Be Complete

Every hero must fit within the available heaven while all sacred laws hold. The puzzle is complete only when the arrangement preserves every story without contradiction.

## Campaign Structure

Each island is a chapter of history with its own dead, visual identity, puzzle rules, and divine landmark. The divine landmark should introduce a memorable mechanical constraint as well as anchor the island's illustration.

Possible chapter motifs:

- The Forge God's ash-black island: oaths, sacrifice, blades, and stars joined by unbroken chains.
- The Tide God's drowned archive: ships, kinship, currents, and threads that must follow tidal paths.
- The Gate God's twilight threshold: rivals, exile, passage, and constellations facing across a gate.
- The Hearth God's amber sanctuary: families, mercy, inheritance, and close-knit constellation chains.
- The Raven God's high observatory: betrayal, memory, distance, and non-crossing threads.

Completing an island should visibly restore it: darkness recedes, stars brighten, threads draw in, the landmark awakens, and the chapter joins the wider Tapestry map.

## Narrative Moment

When the player places a hero, play one short remembered line from that life. It should be intimate and specific, not exposition-heavy. Examples:

- "I held the bridge until the sea took it."
- "My sister kept the lamp burning."
- "I knelt so the city could stand."

When the constellation is complete, reveal a poetic record title such as `The King Who Knelt`, `The Sisters at the Gate`, or `The Lantern-Bearer's Last Voyage`. Preserve completed titles on the campaign tapestry as the player's authored archive.

## Sprite Inventory

### Core Board

- Empty sky-cell tile, with quiet star-dust variation.
- Selectable star node in inactive, hover, selected, valid, invalid, and completed states.
- Star-quality glyphs for courage, wisdom, sacrifice, love, betrayal, oath, grief, and mercy.
- Hero soul marker, with an unplaced silhouette and a placed luminous form.
- Connection thread in inactive, preview-valid, preview-invalid, active, and completed states.
- Thread crossing warning marker.
- Dead or unavailable heaven cell, presented as a sealed patch of darkness rather than construction debris.
- Placement cursor or ritual seal.

### Constellation And Record

- Constellation outline fragments for blade, crown, ship, beast, lantern, gate, and abstract sigil families.
- Completed constellation glow and subtle particle burst.
- Hero portrait medallion or silhouette frame.
- Relationship markers for companion, rival, family, enemy, and oath-bound souls.
- Record plaque or parchment panel for a completed constellation name.
- Short memory-line speech ribbon or subtitle frame.

### Divine Landmarks

- Forge altar or anvil constellation landmark.
- Tide shrine, moon pool, or shipwreck landmark.
- Gate arch landmark with two illuminated mouths.
- Hearth brazier or sanctuary landmark.
- Raven observatory, eye, or high tower landmark.
- Generic celestial shrine, beacon, and boundary-stone variants for future islands.

### Island And Campaign Map

- Island silhouettes in dormant darkness and restored illustrated states.
- God sigils for each island.
- Sea-of-night background tiles, clouds, aurora, and distant star fields.
- Tapestry map thread segments that connect completed chapters.
- Chapter seal in locked, available, in-progress, and complete states.
- Completed chapter miniature showing its restored celestial scene.

### Interface And Feedback

- Keeper's hand, quill, or astronomical instrument cursor.
- Sacred-law icons for shape, meaning, boundary, relationship, and completeness.
- Validity indicators: quiet approval glow, warning halo, and contradiction mark.
- Completion seal and archival stamp.
- Undo, redo, inspect-memory, and reveal-record icons.
- Optional accessibility variants with high-contrast quality glyphs and relationship symbols.

## Audio Direction

- A soft star chime for a valid placement.
- A restrained low dissonance for a violated law.
- A brief, dry spoken memory line when placing a hero.
- Thread-weaving shimmer when a connection resolves.
- A fuller choral or wind swell when a constellation receives its name.
- Island-specific ambience that grows richer as its chapter is restored.

## Design Test

For future mechanics, art, and copy, ask: does this make the player feel like they are carefully preserving a person's story in the heavens? If it instead reads as zoning, production, or city optimization, reframe or reject it.
